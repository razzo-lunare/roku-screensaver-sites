EARTH

Our home planet, in changing light.

A quiet view of Earth with shifting sunlight, warm city lights, the Moon's changing phase, subtle stars and your local time and date.

Calculated sunlight and offline ISS tracking

Earth's day/night boundary follows the device's UTC clock and refreshes each minute. The Moon's phase follows the date; its illuminated limb faces the same projected Sun direction as Earth.

ISS positions are calculated on the Roku using SGP4 and bundled, dated CelesTrak orbital elements. Playback makes no network requests. The station and guide hide outside seven days of the element date, and new elements require an updated app build. Maneuvers and changes in drag can make predictions less accurate before that limit; the window is not an accuracy guarantee. These are predictions, not spacecraft telemetry.

A scene for an idle television

Choose star twinkle, reduced motion, the ISS orbit line and Real time or 2x, 5x and 10x ISS previews in the separate screensaver settings. Faster previews affect only the ISS, not Earth lighting, Moon phase or the clock. Reduced motion freezes the station. Use the Roku remote to dismiss playback.

The view stays centered on a fixed sample camera at 25° N, 95° W; no user location is collected. Globe maps and city lights are historical imagery, not live weather or electricity data. The Moon is an illustrative inset with approximate lighting, and the ISS is enlarged for readability. Store availability is not confirmed.

Images, astronomy and licenses

Earth: NASA Goddard Blue Marble 2002 cloud-free imagery. City lights: NASA Earth Observatory, Black Marble 2016. Moon: NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit 2025 (LRO). ISS artwork: a NASA/JSC IGOAL rendering with AI-assisted background removal, not a validated spacecraft model. Stars and guide strokes are original procedural artwork. Inclusion does not imply endorsement.

Solar lighting uses USNO approximate coordinates and sidereal time. Lunar phase uses a low-order Sun/Moon model. Offline ISS propagation adapts satellite.js 7.1.0 (MIT); source elements, their epoch and limitations are in the bundled orbit record.
