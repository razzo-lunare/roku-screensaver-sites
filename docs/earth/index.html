In development · Standalone Roku screensaver

EARTH

Our home planet, in changing light.

A quiet view of Earth with shifting sunlight, warm city lights, the Moon's changing phase, subtle stars and your local time and date.

Calculated sunlight and offline ISS tracking

Earth's day/night boundary follows the device's UTC clock and refreshes each minute. The Moon's phase follows the date; its illuminated limb faces the same projected Sun direction as Earth.

ISS positions are calculated on the Roku using SGP4 and bundled, dated CelesTrak orbital elements. Playback makes no network requests. The station and guide hide outside seven days of the element date, and new elements require an updated app build. Maneuvers and changes in drag can make predictions less accurate before that limit; the window is not an accuracy guarantee. These are predictions, not spacecraft telemetry.

A scene for an idle television

Choose star twinkle, reduced motion, the ISS orbit line and Real time or 2x, 5x and 10x ISS previews in the separate screensaver settings. Faster previews affect only the ISS, not Earth lighting, Moon phase or the clock. Reduced motion freezes the station. Use the Roku remote to dismiss playback.

The view stays centered on a fixed sample camera at 25° N, 95° W; no user location is collected. Globe maps and city lights are historical imagery, not live weather or electricity data. The Moon is an illustrative inset with approximate lighting, and the ISS is enlarged for readability. Store availability is not confirmed.

Images, astronomy and licenses

Earth: NASA Goddard Blue Marble 2002 cloud-free imagery. City lights: NASA Earth Observatory, Black Marble 2016. Moon: NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit 2025 (LRO). ISS artwork: a NASA/JSC IGOAL rendering with AI-assisted background removal, not a validated spacecraft model. Stars and guide strokes are original procedural artwork. Inclusion does not imply endorsement.

Solar lighting uses USNO approximate coordinates and sidereal time. Lunar phase uses a low-order Sun/Moon model. Offline ISS propagation adapts satellite.js 7.1.0 (MIT); source elements, their epoch and limitations are in the bundled orbit record.

These are approximate orbital predictions, not spacecraft telemetry. Source
elements age; later maneuvers and changes in atmospheric drag are unknown.
The ISS and guide hide outside seven days before/after the element epoch.
That window is an application freshness policy, not an accuracy guarantee.
Updating the orbit requires a new app build containing updated elements.
TEME kilometres are converted to Earth-fixed using Greenwich mean sidereal
time; UTC approximates UT1, with no polar motion/EOP corrections or TDB claim.

Real time is the default ISS speed and starts at the current calculated position
using device UTC. The saved speed slider also offers 2x, 5x and 10x previews of
predicted future positions. Every activation starts again at the current time;
faster previews reset to current time after advancing six hours ahead. Reduced
motion freezes the station display. The icon is enlarged; the full orbit guide
is an approximate instantaneous orbital plane, not an exact future ground track.
The camera still matches the fixed sample Earth view, not the user's location.
Eclipse darkening uses an approximate cylindrical Earth shadow and the same
calculated Sun direction as the globe, at the ISS prediction epoch; the icon's
surface shading remains illustrative, not actual spacecraft attitude. The ISS
artwork is an AI-edited derivative of a NASA/JSC IGOAL model rendering, not an
unaltered NASA image. Small details may differ from the source; current model
configuration and exact structural accuracy are not independently validated. Earth
lighting and lunar phase have independent implementations and accuracy limits.
The orbit guide can be hidden independently in settings.

EARTH playback makes no network requests. Orbital data, images and software
are bundled with the app. No external service receives playback timestamps,
preferences, location or device identifiers from EARTH. There is no project
backend, account, key or paid service. The developer's optional host-side orbit
refresh downloads elements from CelesTrak before building; the Roku never does.
Only complete local prediction batches are used; interpolation stops at their
boundaries. An expired or failed prediction hides the ISS and guide while the
rest of the screensaver continues. An ISS behind Earth is naturally hidden.
Predictions remain in activation memory; there are no response downloads/cache.

Earth's day/night boundary recalculates at startup and once per UTC minute
using USNO approximate solar coordinates and Greenwich mean sidereal time:
https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/sun_approx
https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/GAST
UTC approximates UT1; this is not TDB ephemeris data. No polar motion, EOP,
atmospheric refraction or physical twilight-scattering accuracy is claimed.
The night-side city lights are a historical 2016 composite. The view remains
fixed at the sample camera (25 N, 95 W), not a detected user location. ISS
preview speed does not accelerate Earth lighting. A dim neutral globe without a solar boundary
remains during startup or if initial dynamic lighting fails; the prior usable frame
is retained after a later failure. Solar calculations work offline.
